Before You Give NURP Your Money, I Believe You Should See What Happened to Mine.

I spent almost exactly three years using NURP's automated trading programs.

Some algorithms made money for a time. Some lost money. Some made substantial profits before suffering much larger losses. Surviving capital was sometimes moved from one NURP strategy into another.

This website documents what happened using my brokerage records, transaction histories, NURP communications and other contemporaneous evidence.

You don't have to take my word for it. The underlying records are available throughout this site.

WHAT THE RECORD ESTABLISHES

Five facts to keep in view before the details.

1. I gave NURP's programs a substantial real-world trial.

I was a customer for almost exactly three years and used numerous NURP trading strategies. The reconstructed broker-level result shown below is $1,426,748.43 in trading-capital loss, plus $178,037 in reconstructed net NURP fees.

2. This was not the result of one algorithm.

My NURP experience included FED, three Yellen installations, Pelosi, Dalios, Gold, Odyssey, Argos, All Weather, Talos 5X and Talos 20X. Some were separate accounts; some reused or received capital from earlier strategies. That chronology matters because simply adding every strategy loss would count portions of the same money more than once.

3. For the Talos and Argos programs I used, NURP required Global Next Trade (GNT).

I did not independently choose GNT. In August 2026, NURP reported that across the GNT follower accounts it analyzed, customers retained about 30% of master-account performance, 89% of fills were worse than the master, and approximately 70% of expected returns were lost to execution costs. Those were NURP's portfolio-level findings—not a calculation of my individual damages.

4. Both Talos accounts ultimately went to zero. Both Argos accounts suffered heavy losses.

Talos 5X and Talos 20X were later NURP automated programs using leveraged structures. Leverage can increase gains, but it can also magnify losses.

5. You do not have to take my word for any of this.

This site is built around broker statements, account histories, transactions, emails, NURP presentations, NURP communications and other contemporaneous records. I distinguish documented facts, reconstructed calculations, customer recollections, third-party accounts and unresolved questions throughout the site.

WHY I STAYED

If Things Were Going Badly, Why Did I Stay?

Because they weren't always going badly.

Some of these algorithms made substantial money before they lost money. FED generated approximately $221,448 in cumulative profits through 2024 before the later loss cycle overwhelmed those gains. My earlier Argos account was profitable. My later GNT Argos accounts generated substantial profits during 2025. Gold made money. Even Talos 5X and Talos 20X initially made money.

Those profitable periods reinforced my confidence and help explain why I continued.

The complete story isn't that nothing ever worked. It is what happened to the money over the entire relationship.

The algorithms weren't nearly as good at making money as NURP was at marketing them.

As you read the chronology that follows, notice how compelling NURP's marketing was. The language was confident, specific and reassuring. Then compare those words with what actually happened to my accounts.

WHAT IS A NURP ALGORITHM?

A Computer Program Was Supposed to Do the Trading for Me.

An algorithm is simply a computer-based trading program designed to decide when to open, manage and close trades automatically. I did not sign up because I wanted to become a professional forex trader. The appeal was almost the opposite: sophisticated automated systems would make and manage trading decisions while I went on with my life.

That automation expectation matters later. If a system is sold as automated, a basic question is what the customer is still expected to monitor personally—broker changes, master-account changes, disconnected trades, execution differences or failures in the safeguards that are supposed to limit losses.

THE GNT CHRONOLOGY

The Execution Problem Was Not Only an August 2026 Discovery.

By January 2026, NURP was telling customers that latency and slippage at GNT were causing customer accounts to diverge from master-account results and that it had been communicating with GNT about the problem for weeks.

NURP's August 2026 communication later reported the portfolio-level findings shown above and said it had decided to withdraw its own funds from GNT. The exact date of that internal decision remains unresolved in the records I currently have.

THE QUESTIONWhen did NURP decide its own money should come out of GNT—and when were customers given enough information to protect theirs?
THE ORPHAN-TRADE PROBLEM

A Broker/Master Transition Created a Separate Communication Problem.

NURP sent a communication concerning a master-account/broker change and customer action. I did not receive or see it at the time. Trades already open in my account were left behind when the master arrangement changed. We later referred to these as orphan trades: positions opened through the copying system that were no longer connected to the master strategy in the normal way and could remain open without the intended management or closing instruction.

On March 27, 2026, I wrote to NURP that I had not been directly informed that the master account had changed brokers three months earlier and that the resulting losses exceeded $50,000. Later reconstruction identified dozens of affected positions and substantial losses, but the precise causal allocation remains an open evidence question.

Customer recollection: another NURP customer I knew, Laura B., also did not receive the communication. I preserve that only as recollection unless documentary evidence establishes it.
THE QUESTIONIf customer action was necessary to protect automated trades during a transition, what system existed to make sure affected customers actually received and acted on that information?
TALOS 5X — THE TERMINAL EVENT

September 8 Still Showed an Active Account. The Later Report Showed Zero.

On September 8, 2026, my Talos 5X account showed 280 open positions, 94.05 lots of exposure, an account balance of $100,331.83 and approximately $92,732 in floating losses. The account also displayed substantial broker credit; separating that credit from customer capital produces approximately $7,600 in reconstructed customer economic equity at that snapshot.

The account later reached zero. GNT said rapid market movement and negative floating P&L caused the account to reach a 90% margin stop-out threshold, triggering sequential automatic liquidation, after which broker credit was removed.

Status: I requested the underlying deal records, transaction ledger, account journal and server records needed to reconstruct the terminal event independently. The broker's explanation is preserved as its explanation; the underlying records remain the evidence needed to test it.

THE QUESTIONTalos 20X reached zero. Talos 5X reached zero. What risk-control mechanism was supposed to prevent that outcome?
TESTING AND THE LEARNING CURVE

How Much Real-World Testing Occurred Before Customer Money Was Put at Risk?

NURP presented backtesting, historical drawdowns, live testing and increasingly sophisticated risk-control language. But customer results depend on more than a backtest or a master account. They also depend on broker execution, spreads, slippage, latency, connections and the risk mechanisms actually operating in the customer's account.

Before putting money into a trading program today, I would want to know how long that exact strategy had traded live, how many actual customers had used it, what their median results were, what the worst real-world drawdown was, and whether any customer account had reached zero.

THE QUESTIONWere important problems being discovered during controlled testing—or after customers' money was already at risk?

I cannot establish that NURP knew a particular algorithm was destined to fail. My question is whether its development, broker testing, risk controls and monitoring were adequate before substantial customer capital was exposed.

WHAT THE PUBLIC ACCOUNTS ESTABLISH — AND WHAT THEY DO NOT

These reports do not prove that every NURP customer experienced the same thing. They do not establish that the same cause produced every loss. And they do not tell us what percentage of NURP's entire customer population was profitable or unprofitable.

They establish something narrower: my experience was not the only publicly reported experience involving severe drawdowns, major losses, reversals after profitable periods or accounts reportedly reaching zero.

THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

My results don't depend on these reviews.

The customer accounts provide context. They are not the evidence used to calculate my losses. My financial reconstruction comes from brokerage statements, transaction histories, deposits and withdrawals, NURP invoices and receipts, account records and contemporaneous communications.

If every customer review on this site disappeared tomorrow, the documented financial history of my accounts would remain unchanged.

The public accounts matter for a different reason. After reconstructing what happened to my accounts, I wanted to know whether anyone else was publicly reporting anything similar.

They were.

What those reports still cannot tell us is how representative those experiences were across NURP's entire customer population.

ACCOUNTABILITY

When an Algorithm Failed, What Happened Next?

In my experience, the business kept moving. The losses did not.
