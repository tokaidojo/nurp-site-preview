Before You Give NURP Your Money, I Believe You Should See What Happened to Mine.

I spent almost exactly three years using NURP's automated trading programs.

Some algorithms made money for a time. Some lost money. Some made substantial profits before suffering much larger losses. Surviving capital was sometimes moved from one NURP strategy into another.

This website documents what happened using my brokerage records, transaction histories, NURP communications and other contemporaneous evidence.

You don't have to take my word for it. The underlying records are available throughout this site.

WHY I STAYED

If Things Were Going Badly, Why Did I Stay?

Because they weren't always going badly.

Some of these algorithms made substantial money before they lost money. FED generated approximately $221,448 in cumulative profits through 2024 before the later loss cycle overwhelmed those gains. My earlier Argos account was profitable. My later GNT Argos accounts generated substantial profits during 2025. Gold made money. Even Talos 5X and Talos 20X initially made money.

Those profitable periods reinforced my confidence and help explain why I continued.

The complete story isn't that nothing ever worked. It is what happened to the money over the entire relationship.

The algorithms weren't nearly as good at making money as NURP was at marketing them.

As you read the chronology that follows, notice how compelling NURP's marketing was. The language was confident, specific and reassuring. Then compare those words with what actually happened to my accounts.

WHAT IS A NURP ALGORITHM?

A Computer Program Was Supposed to Do the Trading for Me.

An algorithm is simply a computer-based trading program designed to decide when to open, manage and close trades automatically. I did not sign up because I wanted to become a professional forex trader. The appeal was almost the opposite: sophisticated automated systems would make and manage trading decisions while I went on with my life.

That automation expectation matters later. If a system is sold as automated, a basic question is what the customer is still expected to monitor personally—broker changes, master-account changes, disconnected trades, execution differences or failures in the safeguards that are supposed to limit losses.

THE BROADER CUSTOMER QUESTION

If These Algorithms Did This to My Accounts, What Happened to Everyone Else?

My records establish what happened to my accounts. They do not establish what happened to every NURP customer.

But after reconstructing nearly three years of my own trading, another question became impossible for me to ignore:

Was my experience unusual?

That sounds like a question customer-performance data should answer. It turned out to be considerably harder to answer than I expected.

WHAT THE PUBLIC ACCOUNTS ESTABLISH — AND WHAT THEY DO NOT

These reports do not prove that every NURP customer experienced the same thing. They do not establish that the same cause produced every loss. And they do not tell us what percentage of NURP's entire customer population was profitable or unprofitable.

They establish something narrower: my experience was not the only publicly reported experience involving severe drawdowns, major losses, reversals after profitable periods or accounts reportedly reaching zero.

THE IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

My results don't depend on these reviews.

The customer accounts provide context. They are not the evidence used to calculate my losses. My financial reconstruction comes from brokerage statements, transaction histories, deposits and withdrawals, NURP invoices and receipts, account records and contemporaneous communications.

If every customer review on this site disappeared tomorrow, the documented financial history of my accounts would remain unchanged.

The public accounts matter for a different reason. After reconstructing what happened to my accounts, I wanted to know whether anyone else was publicly reporting anything similar.

They were.

What those reports still cannot tell us is how representative those experiences were across NURP's entire customer population.

THE QUESTIONWhat percentage of NURP customers actually made money?
as One Customer. What Were the Same Algorithms Doing in Other Customer Accounts?

My records establish what happened to my accounts. They do not tell me how many other NURP customers suffered comparable losses.

But these were automated trading algorithms. Customers using the same NURP algorithm were not independently making thousands of unrelated trading decisions; they were relying on the same underlying automated strategy. When that strategy entered a losing trade or suffered a serious loss cycle, the consequences therefore were not necessarily confined to my account. Other customers following the same strategy could be exposed to the same underlying trades, although their exact results could differ because of timing, broker execution, account size and other factors.

That makes the broader question impossible to ignore: when one of these algorithms suffered a catastrophic loss cycle, how many customer accounts were being damaged at the same time?

I could see my accounts. NURP could see something customers could not: the much larger picture across its customer base.

CUSTOMER VISIBILITY

There was another limitation from the customer's side. NURP controlled its Community chat, including what remained visible there. In my experience, customer-to-customer visibility was therefore largely taking place inside a forum controlled by the company itself. When an algorithm suffered serious losses, I did not see anything approaching the amount of sustained discussion or visibility that the scale of the event seemed to warrant.

That matters because customers could see their own losses, but they could not independently use NURP's forum to assemble the larger picture of what was happening across the customer base.

One customer losing a substantial amount of money is one story. An automated trading system producing serious losses across a large customer population at approximately the same time would be something very different.

NURP has represented a customer base numbering in the thousands. If thousands of customers were using these systems, even a fraction experiencing losses comparable to mine could represent an extraordinary amount of customer losses. I cannot responsibly calculate that number from my records, and I will not pretend that I can.

But NURP had access to information I did not: how many customers were running each algorithm, what those customer accounts actually experienced, how many suffered severe drawdowns, and how many were ultimately profitable or unprofitable.

That is the information I would want to know if I were considering becoming a NURP customer today.

THE QUESTIONIf the same algorithms were producing devastating losses across many customer accounts, who inside NURP knew the scale of what was happening—and what did they do with that knowledge?

The customer-wide data that could answer this is straightforward: how many live customer accounts were profitable or unprofitable, the typical customer return after actual execution and costs, typical and worst losses from prior highs, how many customers suffered losses greater than 20%, 50% or 75%, how many accounts approached or reached zero, and how closely customer accounts tracked the master accounts.

ACCOUNTABILITY

When an Algorithm Failed, What Happened Next?

In my experience, the business kept moving. The losses did not.

The progression repeatedly moved forward: FED → Yellen → newer systems → All Weather → Talos. The explanations and technology changed. New algorithms arrived. New safeguards were described. New opportunities were presented. My losses remained.

That pattern became particularly concrete in September 2025. After a sharp gold move drove my three All Weather accounts into stop losses—accounts that ultimately recorded a combined 2025 trading loss of $380,143.09—NURP said the stop-loss safeguard had worked as intended, said it was fast-tracking improvements, and in the same communication announced Talos as its newest algorithm for additional diversification and risk balancing.

Twenty-two days later, $164,872.73 of the capital remaining in those three All Weather accounts was moved into Talos 5X and Talos 20X. Both Talos accounts ultimately reached zero.
