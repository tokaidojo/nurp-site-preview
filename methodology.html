How I checked the numbers

Why the Accounting Is More Careful Than the Headline

The purpose of the methodology is to prevent a large loss from becoming an exaggerated one.

Money moved between accounts

If $50,000 moved from one of my accounts to another, I count it as the same $50,000—not another $50,000 invested.

Broker credit was not my money

Some leveraged accounts displayed large credit balances supplied by the broker. I exclude those credits when reconstructing my actual economic position.

A problem can be real without becoming a second damages number

If affected trades are already inside broker-account depletion, adding them again would double count the same financial loss.

I change the public calculation when better records require it

That happened with OX on September 22. New broker statements changed part of the reconstruction, so the earlier public figure was retired rather than defended.

What the labels mean

Confirmed: directly supported by evidence. Reconstructed: calculated from evidence but not stated as one authoritative total. Open: evidence is not sufficient yet.

Outcome is not automatically causation

A losing account establishes a financial outcome; it does not automatically establish why the loss occurred. Where the records support a dollar amount but do not establish cause, I keep those questions separate. The same standard applies to any future claim that my own actions caused a loss: the claim should be tied to a specific action, account, time period and supporting record.

About this record. This site documents my experience using records available to me. I distinguish broker records and company statements from my recollections and from calculations reconstructed from account data. Where the evidence does not answer a question, I say so. If new records materially change a factual statement or calculation, I will correct it and date the change.
