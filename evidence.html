HOW TO READ THE EVIDENCE

Every important exhibit should answer three questions first

1. What am I looking at?
A plain-English description of the document.
2. What does it show?
The fact or issue the record supports.
3. What does it not prove?
The limitation, so the evidence is not asked to prove more than it actually does.

After that, the underlying source is available for readers who want to go deeper.

D

NURP's own findings about GNT execution

What it is: NURP communications reporting the results of its analysis of customer accounts using GNT.

What it shows: NURP reported that GNT customer accounts retained only about 30% of master-account performance, that roughly 70% of expected returns were lost to execution costs, and that 89% of customer fills were worse than the master.

Why it matters: GNT was the broker NURP required customers to use for the Talos and Argos strategies at issue in my experience. That makes the quality of execution—and NURP's selection, vetting and monitoring of that required broker—an important part of the record.

What it does not prove: These portfolio-level findings do not establish that 70% of my personal losses were caused by execution problems, and I do not use them as a personal damages formula.

I

The orphan-trade episode

In plain English: In this situation, an orphan trade means a trade that had been opened through the copying system but, after a master-account or broker transition, was no longer connected to the master strategy in the normal way. The trade could remain open in the customer account without receiving the intended management or closing instruction from the strategy that originally opened it.

What the evidence shows: I contemporaneously reported more than $50,000 in losses from the problem. A recovered March 3 working spreadsheet lists 61 positions across the two Argos accounts; 48 rows are explicitly labeled “Orphaned” and 13 are labeled “Open.” The 48 explicitly labeled rows total approximately –$16,575.12 at the values shown. Later broker-history reconstruction identified a broader candidate population with substantially larger combined losses.

What it does not prove: The current evidence does not establish that every candidate position was an orphan trade or support adding a separate orphan-trade damages figure on top of the GNT depletion. I therefore do not add one.
