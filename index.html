WHY I AM PUBLISHING THIS

I Stayed With NURP for Years. I Wish I Had Been Able to Read a Record Like This Before I Started.

I did not begin this relationship expecting to become an investigator of my own trading accounts. I expected the algorithms, account connections and broker relationships to work as represented. After nearly three years, I decided the only responsible way to understand the experience was to reconstruct it from the records. This website is the result. My purpose is to make that record understandable to someone who may be considering NURP today.

I am not asking a reader to accept every interpretation I have. I am asking the reader to look at what happened, look at the records, and decide what those facts mean for their own decision.

A TURNING POINT

Then NURP Told Customers Something Remarkable About GNT

In August 2026, NURP reported the results of its own analysis of Global Next Trade, one of the brokers being used for NURP strategies. NURP said GNT customer accounts retained only about 30% of the master account's performance, that roughly 70% of expected returns were lost to execution costs, and that 89% of customer trades were executed at worse prices than the corresponding master trades.

Plain English: the master account generated the trades and customer accounts were supposed to copy those same trades. NURP's analysis said the customer copies at GNT were getting substantially worse results than the trades they were copying.

I do not use NURP's 70% figure as a formula for my personal damages. But after years of trading, this disclosure was highly relevant to understanding my results.

There is another important part of that story: GNT was not simply a broker I independently selected. For the Talos and Argos strategies at issue in my experience, GNT was the broker NURP required customers to use. That makes NURP's later execution findings especially significant and raises a basic question: what responsibility did NURP have to vet and monitor the execution quality of a broker its customers were required to use?

THE RISK-CONTROL QUESTION

NURP Had Presented a 20% Maximum-Drawdown Cap. Later, Both of My Talos Accounts Went to Zero.

In July 2025, NURP presented a leveraged program called AFT and stated that maximum drawdown was capped at 20% of the initial account size.

Drawdown simply means loss from the account's starting or prior value. A statement about a maximum drawdown matters because it tells a customer something about how much of the account may be at risk.

Existing capital was later moved into newer NURP strategies called Talos 5X and Talos 20X. Both ultimately went to zero. I am not claiming that the earlier presentation, by itself, proves Talos was contractually governed by the same 20% cap. That is precisely the unresolved question: what written risk-control terms actually governed Talos?

WHY I THINK THE DETAILS MATTER

This Wasn't Just One Bad Trade

Across the relationship, the record includes large broker-account losses, substantial fees, a broker execution problem later identified by NURP, an earlier episode in which I reported trades becoming orphaned after an account transition, questions about risk controls, and the final failure of the Talos accounts.

What is an “orphan trade”? In this situation, it means a trade that had been opened through the copying system but, after a master-account or broker transition, was no longer connected to the master strategy in the normal way. The trade could remain open in the customer account without receiving the intended management or closing instruction from the strategy that originally opened it.

Some of those events are fully quantified. Others are only partially resolved. I have deliberately kept those categories separate rather than forcing every problem into one damages number.

WHATEVER THE EXPLANATION

Start With the Result

I was a NURP customer for approximately three years. I paid substantial fees for NURP's algorithms. I placed substantial capital into accounts where those algorithms traded. I followed the broker arrangements associated with the strategies, including using GNT for the Talos and Argos strategies. The financial result of that relationship was an enormous loss of my capital.

That result does not, by itself, prove the cause of every dollar lost, and I do not claim that it does. But it is the result that any explanation should ultimately address.

If NURP believes something I did caused these losses, I believe the answer should be specific.

What did I do? When did I do it? Which account or trades did it affect? What records demonstrate the connection? And how much of the loss does that evidence actually explain?

Likewise, where customers were required to use a particular broker for a NURP strategy, a meaningful explanation should address how that broker was selected, what due diligence was performed, how execution quality was monitored, when problems became apparent, and what was done in response.

The purpose of this site is not to prevent NURP from explaining what happened. It is to make sure that any explanation is considered alongside the actual account results and the underlying records.
