What Happened to My Money?

The accounting is complicated because my money moved through several broker accounts over roughly three years. The basic idea is not complicated: I am reconstructing how much of my own capital was economically depleted, while making sure internal transfers are not counted twice and broker-provided credit is not mistaken for my money.

THE SIMPLE VERSION

Three parts of the reconstruction are stable enough to publish now. One is still open.

I am publishing the stable figures separately rather than forcing them into a grand total while the OX Securities history is still being reconciled.

Monaxa — reconstructed
$349,500.34

Economic depletion at Monaxa

The records show $350,000 placed into the Monaxa trading account and $499.66 ultimately returned. That produces reconstructed lifetime economic depletion of $349,500.34.

Important: transfers through the internal Vault are not treated as additional investments. They are movements of existing money.

GNT — reconstructed
$821,912.02

Economic depletion across the GNT accounts

The GNT reconstruction identifies $934,580.15 of external customer capital across the GNT account ecosystem. The two surviving Argos accounts ultimately held $112,668.13, while Talos 5X and Talos 20X were at zero. The resulting reconstructed GNT depletion is $821,912.02.

Internal transfers among the accounts are not counted as new money. Broker credit is also excluded because it was not customer principal.

Why the broker relationship matters: for the Talos and Argos strategies at issue in my experience, GNT was the broker NURP required customers to use. NURP later reported serious execution differences at GNT. I present those execution findings separately from this accounting figure; I do not simply label the entire $821,912.02 as execution damage.

WHY THERE IS NO GRAND TOTAL YET

I could publish a larger number now. I don't think that would be responsible.

The stable Monaxa, GNT and fee figures already show a very large financial impact. But adding a provisional OX calculation and calling the result final would create a headline before the underlying OX reconstruction is finished.

When OX supplied new evidence that changed the earlier calculation, I retired the old public figure. I intend to use the same standard when the remaining records arrive.

HOW I AVOID INFLATING THE NUMBER

Money moving between my own accounts is counted once—not again every time it moves.

I count: identifiable outside capital that entered the broker ecosystem; economic depletion of that customer capital; and NURP fees as a separate direct cost.

I do not count as new investment: internal transfers between my accounts, recycled capital moved from one strategy to another, or broker-provided credit.

I do not separately add: an operational problem such as orphan trades when the resulting loss is already embedded in the broker-account depletion. Doing so could count the same dollars twice.

Broker credit in plain English: some leveraged accounts displayed large amounts of credit supplied by the broker. That credit could make the account's displayed equity look much larger, but it was not my deposited capital. For economic-loss calculations, I separate that broker credit from my own money.
OUTCOME VS. CAUSE

The accounting tells us what happened to the money. It does not automatically tell us why every dollar was lost.

For example, the GNT reconstruction can establish the economic depletion across those accounts. NURP's later GNT execution findings are relevant to understanding what happened, but I do not mechanically apply NURP's portfolio-level percentages to my personal losses.

The same discipline applies to orphan trades, risk controls and any future claim that my own conduct caused a loss. Where causation is not established by the available records, I leave it as an open question rather than turning it into a damages number.
