Orphan Trades — Contemporaneous Reports and Later Broker-Record Corroboration

Status: Partially corroborated causation. A contemporaneous working list has been recovered, but candidate-position P/L is not a verified orphan-trade damages figure.

← Back to the Evidence Room

Evidence: March/April 2026 customer-to-NURP correspondence; recovered March 3 working orphan-trade spreadsheet; September 2026 GNT final-history reconstruction.

Status: Partially corroborated — contemporaneous working list recovered; causal master/copier mapping still pending.

1. What was reported at the time

In March and April 2026, I reported to NURP that a master-account/broker transition had left trades outside their intended management and that the resulting losses exceeded $50,000. The March 27 email stated: “Orphan trade issues cost me over $50k.” The later April communication described the transition as an operational failure and stated that I had incurred at least $50,000 in additional losses.

Evidence treatment: These emails are contemporaneous evidence that the problem and loss were being reported to NURP at the time. They are customer statements as to causation and amount; they are not independent broker proof of which trades were orphaned.

2. What the recovered March 3 working spreadsheet shows

The recovered workbook titled “Orphan Trades 3-3-26” identifies the two Argos GNT accounts and describes them in its headings as containing 32 and 29 orphaned trades, respectively — 61 listed positions in all. At row level, 48 positions are explicitly labeled “Orphaned” and 13 are labeled “Open.” The 48 rows explicitly labeled Orphaned total approximately –$16,575.12 at the values shown in the workbook.

The workbook therefore materially corroborates that a defined orphan-trade problem was being tracked at the account level, but it should not be treated as a complete final loss accounting.

4. Why I do not call the later candidate-position P/L “orphan-trade damages”

An orphan-trade classification is a causal conclusion, not merely a date or loss calculation. To establish it trade by trade, the follower-account history should be reconciled against the relevant master-account history and copier logs. Without that mapping, the later broker-history figures are useful corroboration but not a verified causal damages calculation.

For that reason, I do not add the approximately $100,520 or $111,000 candidate-position figures separately to my overall loss reconstruction. The underlying trading results are already embedded in the GNT account depletion, and adding them again could double-count the same loss.

5. The earlier $16,575.12 working number is now sourced

The recovered workbook contains a total of approximately –$16,575.12 for the 48 rows explicitly labeled “Orphaned.” That explains the earlier working figure that had appeared in my records without a sufficiently identified source.

It remains a contemporaneous snapshot or working figure, not a final orphan-trade loss calculation. The later communications reporting losses above $50,000 and the broader broker-history reconstruction indicate that the March 3 workbook should not be treated as the complete endpoint of the episode.

6. What remains unresolved

Master-account history and copier logs are still needed to determine, trade by trade, which follower positions lost their intended master-strategy management after the transition and what portion of the resulting loss can properly be attributed to that event without estimation.

Plain-English definition: In this situation, an orphan trade means a trade that had been opened through the copying system but, after a master-account or broker transition, was no longer connected to the master strategy in the normal way. The trade could remain open in the customer account without receiving the intended management or closing instruction from the strategy that originally opened it.

What this exhibit supports — and what it does not

The contemporaneous reports, the recovered 61-position working spreadsheet, and the later broker histories together corroborate that an orphan-trade problem was being identified and tracked and that substantial losses were associated with the relevant positions. None of these records alone establishes a complete, trade-by-trade causal damages figure.
