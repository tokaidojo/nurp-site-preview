Orphan Trades — Contemporaneous Reports and Later Broker-Record Corroboration

Status: Partially corroborated causation. A contemporaneous working list has been recovered, but candidate-position P/L is not a verified orphan-trade damages figure.

← Back to the Evidence Room

Evidence: March/April 2026 customer-to-NURP correspondence; recovered March 3 working orphan-trade spreadsheet; September 2026 GNT final-history reconstruction.

Status: Partially corroborated — contemporaneous working list recovered; causal master/copier mapping still pending.

1. What was reported at the time

In March and April 2026, I reported to NURP that a master-account/broker transition had left trades outside their intended management and that the resulting losses exceeded $50,000. The March 27 email stated: “Orphan trade issues cost me over $50k.” The later April communication described the transition as an operational failure and stated that I had incurred at least $50,000 in additional losses.

Evidence treatment: These emails are contemporaneous evidence that the problem and loss were being reported to NURP at the time. They are customer statements as to causation and amount; they are not independent broker proof of which trades were orphaned.

2. What the recovered March 3 working spreadsheet shows

The recovered workbook titled “Orphan Trades 3-3-26” identifies the two Argos GNT accounts and describes them in its headings as containing 32 and 29 orphaned trades, respectively — 61 listed positions in all. At row level, 48 positions are explicitly labeled “Orphaned” and 13 are labeled “Open.” The 48 rows explicitly labeled Orphaned total approximately –$16,575.12 at the values shown in the workbook.

The workbook therefore materially corroborates that a defined orphan-trade problem was being tracked at the account level, but it should not be treated as a complete final loss accounting.

The spreadsheet also explains the previously unresolved working figure of approximately $16,575.12: it is the combined total of the 48 rows explicitly labeled “Orphaned” in the recovered workbook. Because the workbook separately includes 13 rows labeled “Open,” and because later records identify a larger candidate population, $16,575.12 is best treated as a contemporaneous snapshot/working figure rather than the final economic effect of the orphan-trade episode.

Causation control: The missing master/copier mapping is the evidence needed to determine which positions actually became orphaned, when they ceased following the intended master logic, and what portion of later P/L is causally attributable to that event. Until that mapping is recovered, $100,520.28 is a candidate-population result, not a verified orphan-trade damages figure.

4. Why the later candidate-position P/L is not called orphan-trade damages

A later reconstruction of GNT's final histories identified a comparably scaled population of paired follower positions, including 62 positions / 31 paired signals under the pre-February 1 cutoff with combined net P/L of approximately –$100,520.28. That later population is useful corroboration, but without master-account/copier mapping it does not establish that every candidate position was orphaned or that the transition caused the eventual loss.

Neither the contemporaneous “over $50,000” report, the recovered workbook's approximately –$16,575.12 explicitly labeled orphan snapshot, my later working recollection of roughly $56,000, nor the later candidate-position P/L is added separately to the controlled economic-cost total because the underlying trading results are already embedded in GNT depletion.

5. The earlier $16,575.12 working number is now sourced

The recovered March 3 workbook reproduces the approximately –$16,575.12 figure exactly as the combined total of its 48 rows explicitly labeled “Orphaned.” This resolves the source of that earlier working number. It does not establish that $16,575.12 was the complete orphan-trade loss, because the same workbook also contains 13 rows labeled “Open,” the headings describe 61 positions as orphaned trades, and later broker-history reconstruction identifies a broader candidate population.

6. What would still close the causal evidence gap

The decisive records would still be master-account trade history and copier logs that map each follower position to its master signal before, during and after the broker/master transition. Those records would allow the 61-position working list and the later candidate sets to be classified trade by trade and would permit any causal loss to be calculated without estimation.

Public conclusion

The record now supports saying that I contemporaneously reported an orphan-trade problem and losses exceeding $50,000; that a recovered March 3 working spreadsheet identifies 61 positions across the two Argos accounts, with 48 rows explicitly labeled “Orphaned” totaling approximately –$16,575.12 and 13 rows labeled “Open”; and that later broker histories independently reveal a similarly scaled paired follower-position population. The record still does not establish that every listed/candidate position was orphaned or that any one of these working figures is the complete causal damages amount.
