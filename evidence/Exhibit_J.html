2. License fees are already included

The reconstruction includes the documented $19,997 ATA license fee and $20,000 AFT license fee — $39,997 combined. These amounts are already contained within the $178,037 net figure and are not added again separately.

3. Why I treat fees separately from trading losses

Trading losses and fees are different categories of economic cost. Trading results reflect what happened inside the brokerage accounts. Fees are amounts paid for access, licensing, subscriptions or related services.

Keeping them separate makes the reconstruction easier to audit and reduces the risk of counting the same dollars twice.

4. What the number means

The $178,037 figure is my reconstructed net fee cost based on the records currently available: gross fees paid less identified refunds or credits.

It is an accounting figure. It does not, by itself, establish that NURP was required to refund those fees, that any contractual refund condition was satisfied, or that the fees caused the trading losses shown elsewhere on this site.

5. Why the distinction matters

My overall financial experience cannot be understood solely by looking at brokerage-account performance. Substantial fees were also paid during the relationship. At the same time, a fair accounting should recognize refunds and credits rather than presenting only the gross amount charged.

That is why this exhibit reports both sides of the calculation: $199,197 gross fees, less $21,160 in identified refunds or credits, resulting in $178,037 reconstructed net fee cost.

What this exhibit supports — and what it does not

It supports: the reconstructed amount of the net economic cost of NURP fees reflected in the records currently available to me.

It does not prove: legal entitlement to a refund, contractual liability, or causation of any brokerage-account loss. Those are separate questions.
