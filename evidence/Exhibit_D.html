NURP's GNT Execution Findings

Status: Primary NURP disclosure. This exhibit documents NURP's own reported analysis of GNT follower-account execution. The findings are portfolio-level evidence; they are not a formula for calculating my personal damages.

Source: NURP communication concerning GNT account performance and execution.

Status: Primary NURP disclosure — portfolio-level findings, not a customer-specific damages calculation.

1. What NURP reported

NURP reported that its analysis found GNT follower accounts retained only about 30% of master-account performance, meaning roughly 70% of expected returns were lost to execution costs.

NURP also reported that 89% of fills were worse than the master, and described the pattern as consistent across the GNT follower accounts it analyzed.

2. Why the broker relationship matters

For the Talos and Argos strategies at issue in my accounts, GNT was the broker NURP required customers to use. I did not independently select GNT for those strategies.

That context makes the execution findings particularly important. They raise factual questions about what due diligence was performed before GNT was required, how execution quality was monitored, when NURP first identified the problem, and what action followed.

3. What this exhibit supports

This record supports the statement that NURP itself identified a substantial execution-performance disparity between the GNT follower accounts it analyzed and the corresponding master performance.

It also establishes that the execution concern was not merely my later interpretation of my account results; NURP communicated its own findings about GNT execution.

4. What this exhibit does not prove

The reported 70% figure cannot simply be applied to my reconstructed GNT depletion of $821,912.02 to calculate damages. NURP's findings were portfolio-level execution findings, not a customer-specific causal allocation of my losses.

The record therefore supports an execution-quality issue requiring scrutiny, but a separate trade-level analysis would be needed to determine what portion, if any, of my individual economic depletion was caused by execution differences.

5. The accountability questions the record raises

Because GNT was the required broker for the Talos and Argos strategies at issue, the record leaves several important questions for review: what vetting occurred before customers were directed to GNT; what ongoing execution monitoring was performed; when the execution disparity became known; and what steps were taken once it was identified.

Those are questions raised by the documented broker arrangement and NURP's own execution findings. This exhibit does not attempt to supply answers that the available record does not establish.
