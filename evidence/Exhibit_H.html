Public Evidence Room • Exhibit H

Monaxa — Capital Bridge and Lifetime Depletion

Status: Closed / reconciled economic depletion. The available records support $350,000 of capital placed into the Monaxa account, $499.66 ultimately returned, and reconstructed economic depletion of $349,500.34.

← Back to the Evidence Room

Source: Monaxa account history, deposit/withdrawal records and reconstructed capital bridge.

Evidence treatment: Internal transfers are movements of existing capital, not additional customer investment. The accounting result is kept separate from any conclusion about causation.

1. The simple version

The records support a straightforward capital bridge:

$350,000 placed → $499.66 ultimately returned → $349,500.34 reconstructed economic depletion.

This is the economic result reconstructed from the available Monaxa records.

2. What the trading history shows

The reconstructed trading history separates the profitable and losing periods rather than implying that every period lost money.

2024 trades: approximately +$42,138.81 net.

2025 trades: approximately –$391,639.15 net.

Combined, those periods reconcile to approximately –$349,500.34, matching the economic depletion reconstructed from capital placed and capital ultimately returned.

3. Why internal transfers matter

Money can move between accounts or internal facilities without becoming new customer capital. Counting those movements again as fresh deposits would inflate the apparent amount invested and could also inflate a loss calculation.

For that reason, this reconstruction distinguishes external customer capital from internal movements of money already in the trading ecosystem.

4. What this exhibit supports

The records support the reconstructed Monaxa economic depletion of $349,500.34.

They also show why the result should be presented as a lifetime economic reconstruction rather than by selecting only the losing period: the account had profitable trading in 2024 before substantially larger losses in 2025.

5. What this exhibit does not prove

The accounting result does not by itself establish what caused the losses or assign responsibility for them.

Trading involves risk, and a financial loss alone is not proof that a strategy provider, broker, customer action, execution problem or any other specific factor caused the result. Causation must be evaluated separately from the accounting.

6. Source material

The underlying records supporting this reconstruction are preserved separately. Public source copies should redact account identifiers and unrelated personal information while retaining the information needed to understand the capital bridge.
